### The lever the government is pulling for you

Parts 1 through 3 of this series sized levers that banks control: fee pools, tokenised instruments, balance sheet efficiency. Part 4 is different. This lever is not pulled by banks. It is pulled by the sovereign — and it may be the most powerful of the four.

Saudi Arabia has committed over $100 billion to AI, data centre, and digital infrastructure since 2023. The PIF has been recognised as the most active sovereign wealth fund in the world. HUMAIN — launched in May 2025, chaired by HRH Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman — is building the entire AI stack: data centres, cloud, models, applications. The Kingdom has designated 2026 as the Year of Artificial Intelligence.

None of this spending sits in a bank's P&L. But all of it flows *through* banks. Project finance. Trade finance. Treasury. Payroll. Digital wallets. Custody. Every SAR of sovereign AI investment creates banking demand that would not otherwise exist.

The Scoreboard puts the Sovereign Ecosystem lever at **SAR 2.7B (Low) to SAR 13.7B (High)** in annual banking revenue, with a Base case of **SAR 7.1B**. That makes it the second-largest lever after Monetization — and the one with the highest policy certainty.

### 01 · The sovereign AI build is unprecedented in scale

Saudi Arabia is not experimenting with AI. It is industrialising it.

**HUMAIN: the full-stack play.** HUMAIN has announced $23 billion in strategic technology partnerships and a $10 billion venture fund. It has secured 211 plots of land for data centres across the Kingdom, with construction underway on two large campuses comprising 11 data centres, each at 200 MW capacity. The target: 50 MW online by Q4 2025, scaling by 50 MW every quarter through 2026. A joint venture with AMD and Cisco targets 100 MW of phase 1 deployment powered by AMD Instinct MI450 Series GPUs. HUMAIN invested $3 billion in xAI's Series E and signed a joint commitment to build 500+ MW of next-generation AI compute in the Kingdom.

Aramco has signed a term sheet to acquire a significant minority stake, with PIF retaining majority ownership. This is not a government experiment. It is a sovereign-industrial complex being assembled at speed.

**PIF: redirecting the portfolio toward AI.** The PIF 2026–2030 strategy — approved by the Board chaired by HRH the Crown Prince — explicitly pivots toward AI, technology, and digital infrastructure. PIF assets under management reached $912 billion in 2024. The fund has restructured into three portfolios: Vision, Strategic, and Financial. The Vision Portfolio is designed to create competitive domestic ecosystems in six sectors, with AI infrastructure as a named priority.

The shift is not subtle. Saudi Arabia's economy minister stated publicly: "We're reprioritising toward sectors that need it the most, and today it's technology, artificial intelligence."

**The data centre arms race.** Beyond HUMAIN, Saudi Arabia awarded a $2.7 billion contract for the 480 MW Hexagon data centre in Riyadh. Microsoft has committed an estimated $2.2 billion over five years. Google signed a $10 billion partnership with HUMAIN. The Kingdom's ambition is 3–6 GW of AI compute capacity, where each gigawatt requires $30–50 billion in investment.

For context, the UAE — Saudi Arabia's closest peer — launched MGX, a $100 billion AI infrastructure fund backed by Mubadala and G42. The UAE's 5 GW AI Campus was unveiled in May 2025. The two Gulf states are in a regional race to become the sovereign AI hub, and the banking systems of both countries are the financial infrastructure through which this race is funded.

### 02 · How sovereign spending becomes banking revenue

The sovereign AI build creates banking demand through five channels:

**Channel 1: Project finance and construction lending.** NEOM alone has drawn SAR 10 billion in revolving credit facilities from Saudi banks, plus $3 billion in SACE-guaranteed international financing. Across all giga-projects — NEOM, The Red Sea, Qiddiya, Diriyah Gate, Jeddah Central — total project finance requirements run into the hundreds of billions of SAR. AI infrastructure adds a new category: data centre project finance, power purchase agreements, and equipment financing for GPU clusters that cost $2–3 billion per 100 MW facility.

**Channel 2: Corporate and trade finance.** Every giga-project generates a supply chain. Construction materials, technology imports, specialised services — all require letters of credit, trade finance facilities, and working capital lines. The HUMAIN–AMD–Cisco joint venture alone will drive multi-billion SAR import flows through Saudi correspondent banking networks.

**Channel 3: Treasury and cash management.** Vision Realisation Programs push government spending through the banking system. Payroll for hundreds of thousands of construction and technology workers. Vendor payments. Tax and fee collection. The sheer velocity of money through the sovereign ecosystem creates deposit float, FX transaction revenue, and cash management fees.

**Channel 4: Digital infrastructure banking.** SAMA's open banking framework is now live, with licensing of fintech providers underway. STC Bank converted from a payments company to a fully licensed digital bank in January 2025. D360 launched as a digital-only, Sharia-compliant bank. The sovereign push for a digital economy creates new banking rails — and new revenue pools for banks that build the platforms.

**Channel 5: Custody and wealth management.** As PIF portfolio companies mature and list — Aramco's secondary offering, potential NEOM and HUMAIN listings — the custody, brokerage, and wealth management revenue follows. Saudi banks posted record quarterly profits of $6.4 billion in Q1 2026, with net profit up 7.6% year-on-year. The sector earned SR 95 billion in 2025 and is projected to exceed SR 100 billion in 2026. Sovereign ecosystem demand is a meaningful contributor to this trajectory.

### 03 · Four moves that capture sovereign ecosystem value

**Move 1: Build a sovereign programme coverage team.** Most Saudi banks cover government entities through a relationship manager model. The sovereign AI build demands something different — a cross-functional team that can structure project finance for data centres, provide trade finance for GPU imports, offer treasury services for VRP-funded programmes, and advise on IPO readiness for PIF portfolio companies. The bank that organises itself around the sovereign ecosystem as a single client will capture disproportionate share.

**Move 2: Invest in AI to serve AI.** Banks that deploy AI internally — credit decisioning, compliance monitoring, fraud detection, personalised financial products — are better positioned to serve sovereign entities that are themselves AI-native. SDAIA's AI Adoption Framework (November 2025) establishes mandatory governance baselines. Banks that meet these standards signal to government clients that they understand the technology, not just the financing.

**Move 3: Position for digital infrastructure banking.** Open banking, digital wallets, and API-first platforms are not fintech distractions. They are the plumbing through which sovereign digital economy programmes will flow. SAMA's regulatory sandbox has graduated providers like XSquare and NeotTek. The Personal Data Protection Law (PDPL) is fully enforceable. Banks that build compliant digital infrastructure now will be the default counterparty when government digital programmes need a banking partner.

**Move 4: Prepare for the listing pipeline.** PIF has over 225 portfolio companies. Not all will list, but the pipeline is deep. Banks that build equity capital markets capabilities and pre-position for PIF portfolio company listings will capture origination, underwriting, and custody revenue for a decade.

### Base vs High: what separates the two worlds

In the **Base case**, sovereign AI spending proceeds on announced timelines. HUMAIN builds out 500–800 MW of data centre capacity by 2028. Giga-projects draw down existing credit facilities. Open banking grows steadily. PIF lists 5–8 portfolio companies by 2030. Saudi banks earn SAR 7.1B per year in direct revenue from the sovereign ecosystem — a meaningful but not transformational contribution.

In the **High case**, the sovereign AI build accelerates. HUMAIN reaches 1.5+ GW. The AMD–Cisco JV and xAI partnership trigger second-wave data centre builds. PIF executes an aggressive listing programme. SAMA's digital banking framework drives rapid adoption. Banks that have built dedicated sovereign coverage teams and AI-native platforms capture SAR 13.7B per year — nearly double the base.

The difference is not whether the sovereign spends. It will. The difference is whether banks position themselves as infrastructure partners to the ecosystem, or remain passive recipients of deposit float and transaction fees.

### The connection to Levers 1–3

The Sovereign Ecosystem lever is the demand engine behind the other three:

**Lever 1 (Monetization):** Sovereign-backed digital economy programmes create the customer base for embedded finance, BaaS, and new fee pools. HUMAIN's AI applications layer will need banking APIs, payment rails, and financial product distribution.

**Lever 2 (Tokenization):** PIF and CMA are actively exploring tokenised capital markets infrastructure. When the first sovereign sukuk goes on-chain, it will be a government-backed instrument — de-risking the entire asset class for private issuers.

**Lever 3 (Capital Efficiency):** The more capital Saudi banks release through RWA optimisation, the more they can deploy into sovereign ecosystem financing. The two levers are directly linked — capital efficiency funds sovereign lending capacity.

The overlap between L1 and L4 is estimated at SAR 0.3B (Low) to SAR 2.7B (High), already netted in the Scoreboard totals.
