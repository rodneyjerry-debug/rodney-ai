### One number for the board

For four weeks, this series has sized individual levers. Monetization. Tokenization. Capital Efficiency. Sovereign Ecosystem. Each one a separate argument, a separate business case, a separate slide deck competing for board attention.

Part 5 is the slide that makes the other four unnecessary.

The Banking 2030 Scoreboard combines all four levers into a single annual value: **SAR 40.8 billion in the Base case** — after netting the SAR 1.1B overlap between Monetization and Sovereign Ecosystem. That is the incremental revenue and released capital available to Saudi banks that execute across the full stack.

Low case: SAR 19.2B. High case: SAR 66.8B.

The difference between those three numbers is not the economy. It is not oil prices. It is not regulation. It is execution. The banks that build programmes across all four levers — not projects, not pilots, not proof-of-concepts — will compound their way to a 2030 ROE of 25–30% and a collective market capitalisation unlock of **$295 billion or more**.

The banks that pick one lever and ignore the others will wonder why the spread never narrowed.

### 02 · Why the levers compound

The Scoreboard is not an additive model. The levers reinforce each other in ways that make the whole larger than the sum.

**Capital Efficiency funds Monetization.** Every SAR released through RWA optimisation (L3) is a SAR available to invest in the BaaS platforms, data products, and API infrastructure that drive new fee pools (L1). A bank that frees SAR 2.5B of capital and deploys it at a 15% marginal ROE generates SAR 375 million of incremental income — before the fee revenue itself.

**Sovereign Ecosystem creates Monetization demand.** HUMAIN's AI applications layer will need banking APIs, payment rails, and financial product distribution (L1). The hundreds of thousands of workers on giga-project payrolls need digital wallets, remittance services, and consumer lending products. The sovereign build is not just project finance — it is a customer acquisition engine.

**Tokenization enables Capital Efficiency.** A bank that tokenises its sukuk book (L2) gains real-time portfolio visibility and automated compliance — both of which improve RWA calculation accuracy and reduce capital buffers (L3). DLT-based collateral registries eliminate the data quality gaps that cause regulatory capital waste.

**Sovereign Ecosystem accelerates Tokenization.** When PIF or CMA endorses on-chain capital markets infrastructure (L4), it de-risks the regulatory path for private tokenised issuance (L2). Government first, private sector follows.

### 03 · Three scenarios for the board

**Low case: SAR 19.2B / year — the "comply only" path.**
Banks meet regulatory requirements (Basel IV, SDAIA governance framework, open banking). They launch AI pilots. They service sovereign clients through standard corporate banking products. They hold capital buffers at regulatory minimums and tokenise nothing. ROE reaches 21% — respectable, but not enough to close the valuation gap with global peers. Market cap: $212B.

**Base case: SAR 40.8B / year — the "programme" path.**
Banks run coordinated programmes across at least three levers. They have a Monetization unit building data products and BaaS. They run a capital efficiency programme with board-level sponsorship. They bid actively for sovereign ecosystem banking. They explore tokenisation through regulatory sandboxes. ROE reaches 25% — competitive with the best emerging market banking sectors. Market cap: $295B.

**High case: SAR 66.8B / year — the "platform" path.**
Banks transform into AI-native financial platforms. Every origination decision is optimised for risk-return-capital efficiency. Tokenised instruments are production-grade. The bank is embedded in the sovereign AI ecosystem as an infrastructure partner, not a service provider. ROE reaches 30% — world-class. Market cap: $392B.

### 04 · The execution gap

The difference between SAR 19.2B and SAR 66.8B is SAR 47.6B per year. Over five years, that is nearly SAR 240B of cumulative value — or approximately $64 billion.

What separates the scenarios is not technology availability. Every tool referenced in this series — IRB models, tokenisation platforms, agentic AI, digital banking infrastructure — exists today. What separates them is:

**Governance.** Does the board treat AI as a strategic programme with a named owner, a budget, and a target number? Or is it a collection of uncoordinated pilots scattered across business units?

**Integration.** Do the four levers have a single executive sponsor who sees the interactions? Or are Monetization, Tokenization, Capital Efficiency, and Sovereign Ecosystem running in separate silos, each with its own business case and its own governance?

**Speed.** The sovereign is moving fast. HUMAIN went from announcement to 200 MW of construction in under a year. PIF restructured into three portfolios in a single board cycle. SDAIA published a mandatory governance framework in months, not years. The question is whether banks can match that velocity.

### 05 · What a 2030 bank looks like

The bank that executes across all four levers in 2030 looks fundamentally different from a Saudi bank today:

**Balance sheet:** 15–30% lower RWA density through IRB migration, collateral optimisation, and active portfolio management. Capital is a strategic resource, not a regulatory buffer.

**Revenue mix:** 20–30% of non-interest income from data products, embedded finance, BaaS, and Intelligence-as-a-Service. Fee income is recurring, platform-driven, and decoupled from the interest rate cycle.

**Technology stack:** Agentic AI in production across credit decisioning, compliance monitoring, customer service, and portfolio optimisation. Not copilots — autonomous workflows with human oversight at decision boundaries.

**Market position:** Named sovereign ecosystem banking partner for PIF, HUMAIN, and at least two giga-projects. Not a commodity lender — an infrastructure partner.

**Capital markets:** At least one tokenised instrument in production — a sukuk, a deposit token, or a tokenised fund. First-mover in the GCC digital asset ecosystem.

### The Scoreboard is not a forecast

The numbers in this series are not predictions. They are sizing exercises — structured estimates of how large each lever could be under different execution assumptions. The actual outcome for any individual bank depends on strategy, speed, talent, and governance.

What the Scoreboard does is make the opportunity cost visible. Every quarter that passes without a programme in place is a quarter of the SAR 40.8B that lands on someone else's balance sheet — or does not materialise at all.
