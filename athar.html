For the Chief Risk Officer who has been asked how many AI systems the bank runs — and does not yet have a defensible answer.

The problem

AI arrived faster than the governance around it

Not because anyone was careless. Because models, copilots, vendor features and agents entered through a dozen doors at once, and none of those doors reported to the same place.

No single inventory

Procurement knows the vendors. Data science knows the models. Nobody holds the list — and the embedded AI inside SaaS you already bought is on nobody's list at all.

Risk assessed by opinion

Two teams look at the same use case and reach different conclusions, because no written rule turns attributes into a tier. Consistency cannot be evidenced.

Approvals that cannot be reconstructed

The decision was made in a meeting. The evidence was in an attachment. Eighteen months later, nobody can show what was known when it was signed off.

Evidence assembled in a panic

A regulatory request arrives and three people spend two weeks rebuilding a story from email. The answer is usually correct. It is never defensible.

    Why it holds up

    A decision history that cannot be rewritten

    Every approval carries a hash of the one before it. Change any earlier record and the chain no longer verifies. An auditor does not have to take your word that the history is intact — they can check it.
